# Appendix C　Reading the Original Declaration

*Privilege and Freedom: Who Makes the Rules, Who Pays the Price · English draft v0.2 · 17 September 2026*

## How to read this declaration

The *Mainland China Liberty Declaration* is this book's starting point. It asserts equal rights, collective authorization, and the right to withdraw consent. It also makes specific allegations. Principles require public argument; allegations require evidence appropriate to them. Calling an allegation a fact does not complete its verification. Opponents of arbitrary power must allow their own claims to be examined and corrected.

This appendix is a reading guide, not a reprint. Readers can consult the [current Chinese text](https://github.com/my-freedom-foundation/declarations/blob/main/mainland-china-liberty/cn/dec-simplified.md) and [current English text](https://github.com/my-freedom-foundation/declarations/blob/main/mainland-china-liberty/en/dec.md). The two texts used here were retrieved from GitHub on 16 September 2026. Neither body has a date line. This book retains the attribution “2022 declaration,” but the complete Git history has not been checked: the current text is not an authenticated final version from 2022. The signatory list is not reproduced.[Version note L08](93-notes.md#ch12-l08)

The entries use D01–D16 to identify the declaration's sixteen claims. Each **Original claim, paraphrased** states what the declaration alleges, not what the book has established. The annotations explain what the available material supports and what remains open to investigation. An unfinished investigation does not mean an allegation has been disproved.

## The opening: rights, consent, and sweeping generalizations

The declaration grounds equal rights to life, freedom, and the pursuit of happiness in faith. It presents councils, governments, courts, and armies as exercising collectively authorized powers, which persistently abusive rulers can lose. This book carries forward that political principle: people are not state property, and victory creates no perpetual right to rule. Chapter Twelve adds that these rights belong equally to people of different faiths and none; peaceful correction and replacement must become usable institutions.

The opening's claims of over seventy years of world peace, the lowest wages, the hardest work, the greatest risks, and no basic protection cannot serve as comparative findings covering every population. Nor can an invariably kind and peaceful national character be assigned to every Chinese person. Concern for suffering does not depend on unsupported superlatives or assumptions about national nature. The opening's accounts of deaths under lockdown require investigation of particular causes, as D04 does. Faith and moral anger cannot replace that work.

## D01 — Pandemic origins, deliberate release, and a personal order

**Original claim, paraphrased:** The declaration alleges that Xi Jinping personally ordered the worldwide release of a virus produced through military experimentation, causing mass casualties.

Chapter Eleven explicitly presents this responsibility claim as the book's argument while stating that deliberate release and a personal order remain unproven. Historical judgment can draw on mutually supporting evidence; it need not await a signed order or conviction. Laboratory origins, military involvement, deliberate release, and individual orders nevertheless remain separate propositions. Emails released in 2026 establish institutional discussion and a preliminary favorable assessment of some scientific content in Dr. Li-Meng Yan's report, not verification of all its claims. Chinese mentions experiments going out of control in Wuhan; English omits that stage and locates the military laboratory there. These cannot silently become one account.[P01–P03](93-notes.md#ch11-p01)

## D02 — Energy agreements and support for the invasion of Ukraine

**Original claim, paraphrased:** The declaration interprets energy orders during the Beijing Winter Olympics as support for Russia's invasion and alleges a military alliance.

The Chinese amount means hundreds of billions of dollars; English says “multi-billion.” They do not specify the same sum. Contract dates, duration, amounts, payments, performance, and the alleged alliance's legal character remain unverified here. Resources supplied through trade, signatories' purposes, and effects on war decisions require separate examination. Coinciding events alone do not establish causation. Chapter Nine's other cases do not fill these gaps.[Chapter Nine sources and outstanding research](93-notes.md#chapter-nine-how-interests-bind-and-diverge)

## D03 — Material, weapons, and financial assistance to Russia

**Original claim, paraphrased:** The declaration alleges that Xi continued providing this support after sanctions against Russia.

The notes to Chapter Eleven discuss NATO's 2024 statement and US Treasury sanctions findings concerning particular China-based businesses' participation in Russian attack-drone production, distinguishing each institution's assessment. These make assistance a concrete subject of inquiry; they do not complete examination of every transaction or personal authorization alleged here. Weapons, dual-use items, ordinary commerce, financial transactions, and state instructions are distinct. Later assistance does not itself establish who brought about the initial invasion.[P11](93-notes.md#ch11-p11)

## D04 — Lockdowns, harm, and consolidation of power

**Original claim, paraphrased:** The declaration alleges that the pandemic was created to consolidate power, and that lockdowns, repeated testing, and compulsory quarantine caused disasters.

Chapter Ten examines Li Wenliang's reprimand and its withdrawal, alongside political requirements imposed on news and platforms. These support criticism of suppressed warnings and information control. They do not establish a motive for creating the pandemic or complete investigations of denied treatment, food deprivation, and deaths. Chinese refers to disasters in various places; English expands this to “worldwide.” That expansion is not an established finding. Chapter Eleven's protection and accountability proposals supply no missing casualty evidence.[T01–T04](93-notes.md#ch10-t01)

## D05 — Term limits and lifelong power

**Original claim, paraphrased:** The declaration connects removing presidential term limits with an intention to rule for life.

The 2018 amendment removed the two-consecutive-term ceiling. Election records from 2013, 2018, and 2023 establish a third consecutive presidential term. The Chinese text incorrectly gives two years in place of two terms; the English text is correct on this point. The presidency and party offices, term length and limits on reelection, must remain distinct. The rule change merits direct criticism, but it did not itself confer a lifetime office. A personal lifelong plan remains unestablished.[F09–F10](93-notes.md#ch07-f09)

## D06 — A mausoleum and an imperial interpretation

**Original claim, paraphrased:** The declaration connects mausoleum construction, a religious figure's influence, and an intention to restore imperial rule.

Construction, location, funding, and private motives remain unverified here. Chinese describes construction as accomplished; English describes a plan. These are different stages. Even established architectural grandeur would not by itself prove an imperial project. Chapter Seven's examination of public institutions and concentrated power did not establish these allegations.[Chapter Seven scope](93-notes.md#chapter-seven-family-organization-and-supreme-power)

## D07 — Private family allegations and hereditary succession

**Original claim, paraphrased:** This entry infers a predetermined hereditary arrangement from an unverified private family relationship.

This book does not repeat identifying clues or investigate or spread a minor's private identity. The claim has not entered its factual narrative. Hereditary risks can be examined through public appointments, eligibility rules, resource transfers, and constraints on power. Protecting family members' rights does not prevent investigation of officials' actual abuses.

## D08 — Hong Kong's promises and responsibility for repression

**Original claim, paraphrased:** The declaration alleges the destruction of autonomy guarantees and deployment of the PLA and armed police against peaceful protests.

Chapter Eight compares the Joint Declaration, Basic Law, electoral changes, and national-security laws, and examines specific rights disputes. The fifty-year assurance concerns the basic policies stated in the Joint Declaration; it does not forbid every institutional change. Criticism of autonomy and accountability has its own evidentiary basis. Particular military deployments, actions, and command responsibility remain unverified here; the former cannot establish the latter.[HK03–HK04](93-notes.md#ch08-hk03), [HK11–HK15](93-notes.md#ch08-hk11)

## D09 — Xinjiang detention and international crimes

**Original claim, paraphrased:** The declaration alleges detention and persecution in Xinjiang and characterizes them as genocide and crimes against humanity.

The UN's summary of its 2022 human-rights assessment states that arbitrary detention and wider rights deprivation may constitute international crimes, particularly crimes against humanity. This book's account is limited to the summary; the full report has not been reviewed. This qualified assessment is neither a UN ruling of genocide nor a criminal conviction. Factual investigations, different institutions' legal assessments, and court judgments require separate attribution. The declaration's Nazi analogy does not replace that work.[UN summary of the assessment](https://unric.org/en/unric-info-point-library-newsletter-september-2022/)

## D10 — Prison labor and export supply chains

**Original claim, paraphrased:** The declaration alleges a nationwide prison-industrial system using unpaid labor for low-price dumping, violating rights and disrupting markets.

This investigation remains incomplete. It requires particular facilities, voluntariness, pay and punishments, suppliers, products, and transactions. Different labor arrangements cannot be equated by their labels. National scale and price effects need separate data. Chapter Five's criticism of labor relations and power cannot establish the provenance of a particular export.

## D11 — Seizure of power and continuing consent

**Original claim, paraphrased:** The declaration alleges seizure of power through propaganda, infiltration, and armed action, calling the new government illegal.

Chapter Five compares statements on leadership from 1949 and party-government relations from 1953 with the 1954 Constitution. They illuminate the institutional relationship between party leadership and state power. The full history of war and seizure of power remains unexamined here. “Illegal” needs an identified legal standard; it is not an existing judicial finding. The book's political judgment is clear: acquiring state power does not acquire the people's irrevocable consent.[S02–S04](93-notes.md#ch05-s02)

## D12 — The Great Famine and the Cultural Revolution

**Original claim, paraphrased:** The declaration attributes these disasters to human action by the regime.

Chapter Five examines grain institutions, Peng Dehuai's writings, the 1981 resolution, Yang Jisheng's recollections and Xinyang investigation, and material conveying Bian Zhongyun's family's demand for investigation. These support discussion of policy, suppressed correction, and responsibility for violence. Memories, investigative accounts, and retrospective party judgments retain their attribution. National death estimates, complete decision chains, and local implementation remain under study. The book neither combines the disasters' death totals nor describes every injury as planned in advance.[S01–S11](93-notes.md#ch05-s01)

## D13 — Military repression in 1989

**Original claim, paraphrased:** The declaration alleges military suppression of students expressing their demands.

Chapter Six uses a hunger-strike declaration, a US embassy retrospective cable, Ding Zilin's testimony concerning Jiang Jielian's death, and Deng Xiaoping's subsequent speeches. Streets and square, students and residents, direct observation and reported information remain distinct. National casualties and the complete command chain need further investigation; this does not erase recorded shootings or individual victims. Where the declaration focuses on 4 June, the book preserves evidence extending from late 3 June into 4 June.[R08–R12](93-notes.md#ch06-r08)

## D14 — The 610 institution, Falun Gong, and organ sourcing

**Original claim, paraphrased:** The declaration connects the 610 institution, persecution of Falun Gong practitioners, and forced organ harvesting.

Specialized verification remains incomplete. Institutional history, particular detentions and injuries, organ sourcing, and individual responsibility require separate examination. Investigative reports, expert judgments, nongovernmental tribunal opinions, and state-court judgments have different status; shared legal language does not make them interchangeable. Chapter Ten's discussion of information mechanisms did not complete these case investigations.

## D15 — Religious belief and organizational control

**Original claim, paraphrased:** The declaration alleges persecution of Christians, Tibetan Buddhists, and Muslims, and party control within religion, attributing this to fear of faith.

The book defends freedom of belief, including changing one's faith or having none. Particular persecution and organizational arrangements still need examination by religion, region, period, and measure. Individual or collective motives cannot be established by a single explanation. One community's case cannot represent every community's experience; institutional criticism must keep particular victims visible.

## D16 — Birth policy and forced termination of pregnancy

**Original claim, paraphrased:** The declaration alleges forced abortions under birth policy and gives a total of tens of millions.

Chinese refers to family planning; English narrows this to the one-child policy. Their scope is not identical. Policy texts, local coercion, and statistical definitions still require investigation. All abortions cannot be counted as forced abortions; the original total remains unverified. Women's physical and emotional suffering deserves serious investigation, and respect for their experiences requires accurately describing what happened.

## The conclusion: public scrutiny of commitments

The declaration closes by withdrawing recognition from existing governing institutions, seeking a government that protects everyone's basic rights, calling supporters of freedom to unite, and pledging lives, possessions, and honor. These are political positions and commitments, not a record that every citizen has consented. Its repeated claims about lifelong rule, hereditary power, and continuing persecution do not verify the preceding allegations. Words such as “currently” belong to the declaration's narrative, not an investigation of conditions in 2026.

Chapter Twelve develops constraints on everyone who exercises power: equal rights; replaceable representatives; correctable factual claims; responsibility following actual conduct; usable peaceful correction; and answers and protection for ordinary people. These are the book's additional requirements, not quotations retrospectively assigned to the declaration. Opposition to CCP arbitrariness authorizes no persecution of relatives, all party members, or people holding different ideas. Successors must face the same standards.[Chapter Twelve](12-what-freedom-requires.md)

This appendix discusses the declaration's opening, sixteen claims, and conclusion, explaining the basis for each assessment in material available as of 16 September 2026. The investigations remain open. New material may strengthen, narrow, or overturn particular judgments. Incomplete investigation proves neither innocence nor guilt. Freedom need not depend on every earlier formulation remaining unchanged. It requires that people can safely keep asking questions and demand answers from those with power.
