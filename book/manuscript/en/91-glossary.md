# Appendix B　Key Terms

*Privilege and Freedom: Who Makes the Rules, Who Pays the Price · English draft v0.2 · 17 September 2026*

These thirty-five entries follow the book's questions: liberty and restraint first, relationships and interests next, then evidence, judgment, and institutional vocabulary. They explain how the terms are used here. Matching identifiers connect the English and Chinese editions.

## G01 · Privilege（特权）

An institutional advantage enabling some people to exercise power over others who struggle to refuse or demand accountability on equal terms. Wealth, ability, or family assistance alone does not establish such privilege; the question is whether an advantage confers exclusionary power, arbitrary authority, or immunity from accountability. “Presidential privilege” in the Nixon case has a specific legal meaning and cannot be judged illegitimate merely because it shares the word. See [Chapter One](01-born-unequal.md) and [Chapter Twelve](12-what-freedom-requires.md).

## G02 · Liberalism（自由主义）

The book's political standpoint: each person should have equal basic rights, and public power requires authorization, boundaries, and enforceable scrutiny. This standard examines how current rulers, their opponents, and their successors treat dissent. See [Chapter Twelve](12-what-freedom-requires.md).

## G03 · Rights（权利）

Claims to protection, treatment, or freedom of action, together with boundaries that other people and institutions should respect. Written recognition is a beginning; the ability to refuse, speak, seek review, and obtain remedies determines whether a right can be used. See [Chapter One](01-born-unequal.md) and [Chapter Twelve](12-what-freedom-requires.md).

## G04 · Power（权力）

The capacity to influence or determine another person's circumstances, arising through office, organization, resources, or relationships. Possessing that capacity describes an actual relationship. Whether its exercise is justified requires examination of authorization, limits, and responsibility. See [Chapter Five](05-who-owns-the-state.md) and [Chapter Seven](07-family-organization-supreme-power.md).

## G05 · Authorization（授权）

The granting of authority to a person or institution to make decisions within a defined scope. Political authorization should have boundaries, duration, and ways to withdraw or reconsider it. Past support does not mean every subsequent decision has already received consent. See [Chapter Two](02-consent-of-the-governed.md) and [Chapter Twelve](12-what-freedom-requires.md).

## G06 · Consent of the governed（被统治者的同意）

The principle that government's rightful authority rests on the consent of those it governs. Giving it practical force requires answers about participation, dissent, and replacing representatives. Silence, compliance, or a single victory cannot establish these conditions by themselves. See [Chapter Two](02-consent-of-the-governed.md).

## G07 · Equal rights（平等权利）

Equal standing to claim protection and make demands, regardless of birth, wealth, or political loyalty. Equal rights do not require identical life outcomes. They do require reasons for differential treatment that can withstand scrutiny. See [Chapter One](01-born-unequal.md) and [Chapter Twelve](12-what-freedom-requires.md).

## G08 · Accountability（问责）

A relationship in which those exercising power must explain decisions, accept scrutiny, and, where warranted, correct errors and bear responsibility. Access to records, independent review, and enforcement matter. A leader's self-criticism or internal discipline may form part of this process without completing it. See [Chapter Five](05-who-owns-the-state.md) and [Chapter Twelve](12-what-freedom-requires.md).

## G09 · Rule of law（法治）

A condition in which power itself is constrained by law that people can invoke and have enforced. The test includes whether ordinary people can challenge officials through rules, independent adjudication, and effective remedies. See [Chapter Twelve](12-what-freedom-requires.md).

## G10 · Independent review（独立审查）

Examination of material, reasons, and decisions by people or institutions free from the reviewed party's improper control. Independence requires access to evidence, protection in carrying out duties, and conditions for enforcement. Reviewers must also give reasons and accept appropriate scrutiny. See [Chapter Ten](10-who-decides-what-the-public-knows.md) and [Chapter Twelve](12-what-freedom-requires.md).

## G11 · Due process（正当程序）

Procedural safeguards against arbitrary accusation and punishment, including notice of allegations, an opportunity to defend oneself, examination of evidence, and independent, impartial adjudication. These protections extend to people we strongly oppose. Invoking a procedural right is not itself an admission of guilt. See [Chapter Ten](10-who-decides-what-the-public-knows.md) and [Chapter Twelve](12-what-freedom-requires.md).

## G12 · Remedy（救济）

Correction or redress available after an infringement, such as stopping the harm, restoring rights, or obtaining compensation under law. A remedy needs accessible procedures and the means to enforce a decision. Announcing a complaints channel does not establish that victims have received help. See [Chapter Eight](08-hong-kong.md) and [Chapter Twelve](12-what-freedom-requires.md).

## G13 · Hereditary advantage（世袭优势）

Resources, standing, trust, or access to consequential relationships passed through a family. These can affect competition for public office. Kinship alone establishes neither a formal succession rule nor every relative's participation in wrongdoing. See [Chapter One](01-born-unequal.md) and [Chapter Seven](07-family-organization-supreme-power.md).

## G14 · Exit costs（退出成本）

The resources, opportunities, and security someone may lose by leaving a job, organization, market, or relationship of domination. Higher costs can make a formally available exit difficult to use and strengthen dependence. The legal character and degree of coercion of different relationships still require separate judgment. See [Chapter Three](03-slaveholders-in-a-free-republic.md) and [Chapter Nine](09-how-interests-bind-and-diverge.md).

## G15 · Institutional incentives（制度激励）

Conditions created by rules and resource allocation that make one choice more rewarding or another more dangerous. Promotion controlled by superiors, for example, may encourage officials to prioritize those superiors' demands. Incentives help explain tendencies without directly revealing each person's private motives. See [Chapter Four](04-in-the-name-of-equality.md) and [Chapter Five](05-who-owns-the-state.md).

## G16 · CCP, state, and Chinese people（中共、国家与中国人）

CCP means the Chinese Communist Party. State bodies exercise particular public powers; Chinese people have varied experiences, views, and choices. Connections among Party leadership, institutional conduct, and individual responsibility require examination. A ruling organization is not the whole society, and nationality or ethnicity is not evidence of crime or control. See [Chapter Five](05-who-owns-the-state.md) and [Chapter Twelve](12-what-freedom-requires.md).

## G17 · Cooperation（合作）

Participants working together on a particular matter. Shared benefits can sustain cooperation while participants retain different objectives. One instance does not establish a permanent alliance, common organizational membership, or unified command. See [Chapter Nine](09-how-interests-bind-and-diverge.md) and [Chapter Ten](10-who-decides-what-the-public-knows.md).

## G18 · Dependence（依赖）

A relationship in which significant opportunities, income, or security persistently rely on resources another party controls. Dependence may encourage concessions or self-restraint. Its strength depends on alternatives, exit costs, and the other party's actual capacity to exert pressure. See [Chapter Nine](09-how-interests-bind-and-diverge.md).

## G19 · Control（控制）

The substantive capacity to determine, veto, or compel a particular decision or action. Establishing control requires connecting conduct to authority, resources, instructions, or other reliable material. Similar views, benefits received, and funding relationships offer different leads; none alone supplies that connection. See [Chapter Seven](07-family-organization-supreme-power.md) and [Chapter Ten](10-who-decides-what-the-public-knows.md).

## G20 · Conflict of interest（利益冲突）

A situation in which personal, family, or organizational interests may affect the performance of public or professional duties. It calls for disclosure, assessment, and appropriate limits on participation. An interest does not automatically invalidate a judgment, but concealing it obstructs others' assessment. See [Chapter Ten](10-who-decides-what-the-public-knows.md) and [Chapter Eleven](11-pandemic-evidence-and-responsibility.md).

## G21 · Source（来源）

Where material comes from: a person, document, record, or channel through which information is supplied. Its weight varies with the question and depends on opportunity to know, actual content, independence, and interests. Many reproductions of one text can still have a single informational origin. See [Chapter Ten](10-who-decides-what-the-public-knows.md).

## G22 · Evidence（证据）

Material that supports or weakens a particular claim. The existence of a document can establish that something was written without proving every event it describes. Evidentiary weight depends on the relationship between the material and the question being decided. See [Chapter Ten](10-who-decides-what-the-public-knows.md) and [Chapter Eleven](11-pandemic-evidence-and-responsibility.md).

## G23 · Inference（推论）

An explanation of a mechanism, motive, or event drawn from stated material and premises. Confidence can vary, and alternative explanations and new evidence must remain relevant. Sequence, capability, or possible benefit needs further reasoning to establish specific causation. See [Chapter Seven](07-family-organization-supreme-power.md) and [Chapter Nine](09-how-interests-bind-and-diverge.md).

## G24 · Allegation（指控）

A claim that a person or organization committed particular wrongdoing. Readers need to know who makes it, what conduct it concerns, and its basis. Key links in this book's allegation of deliberate viral release and a personal order remain unestablished by the available material; a political position cannot substitute for evidence. See [Chapter Eleven](11-pandemic-evidence-and-responsibility.md).

## G25 · Finding（认定）

A conclusion reached by a court, investigating body, or other identified decision-maker within a particular procedure and body of material. A finding must identify who reached it, what it concerns, and how far it applies. Criminal verdicts, administrative investigations, risk reviews, and the analytical judgments in this book have different standing and cannot be exchanged or extended without limit. See [Chapter Nine](09-how-interests-bind-and-diverge.md) and [Chapter Ten](10-who-decides-what-the-public-knows.md).

## G26 · Source independence（来源独立性）

Whether different materials derive from separate access to relevant knowledge rather than a shared person, announcement, or transmission chain. Independence is assessed claim by claim. Several websites reproducing one document may help locate the text without increasing the number of independent witnesses. See [Chapter Ten](10-who-decides-what-the-public-knows.md).

## G27 · Consensus（共识）

A shared or convergent judgment among participants. Its weight depends on independent inquiry, substantive examination, and the opportunity to dissent. Repetition of one source or agreement left after dissent is suppressed must be distinguished from conclusions reached independently. See [Chapter Ten](10-who-decides-what-the-public-knows.md) and [Chapter Eleven](11-pandemic-evidence-and-responsibility.md).

## G28 · Whistleblower（披露者／吹哨者）

A person reporting risks, failures of duty, or wrongdoing they have learned of or reasonably suspect. The book argues for early preservation of records and protection against retaliation when grounds are reasonable and specific, alongside continued verification. Protecting a source and punishing an accused person require different thresholds. See [Chapter Eleven](11-pandemic-evidence-and-responsibility.md).

## G29 · Anonymity and confidentiality（匿名与保密）

Anonymity concerns whether a person's identity can be discovered; confidentiality concerns who is authorized to access information. Neither guarantees the other. Content, communications records, or devices may reveal identity, while restricted access does not mean the recipient is unaware of the source. See [Chapter Eleven](11-pandemic-evidence-and-responsibility.md).

## G30 · Emergency powers（紧急权力）

Public powers temporarily expanded or specially granted to address serious danger. The book calls for specified purposes, necessity, duration, and review conditions. Even if a risk persists, the continuing need for each restriction must be justified again. See [Chapter Eleven](11-pandemic-evidence-and-responsibility.md) and [Chapter Twelve](12-what-freedom-requires.md).

## G31 · Current framework and historical text（现行制度与历史文本）

A current framework is an arrangement applicable at a specified time, place, and to specified subjects. A historical text records an earlier arrangement. Old constitutions, past tariffs, and appointment records retain their dates and scope; they do not automatically describe today's entire system. The book's revision date is not a guarantee that every arrangement has been rechecked to that date. See [Chapter Six](06-open-markets-political-continuity.md), [Chapter Seven](07-family-organization-supreme-power.md), and [Chapter Nine](09-how-interests-bind-and-diverge.md).

## G32 · United front work（统一战线／统战）

The organizational work described in the CCP regulations cited here, directed toward people outside the Party and addressing common objectives, differences, and allies' interests. It shows that cooperation need not depend on Party membership. A group being listed as a target of this work does not establish that every member is organized or controlled. See [Chapter Ten](10-who-decides-what-the-public-knows.md).

## G33 · Household registration / hukou（户口登记）

A system for registering residents, residence, and movement. The book examines requirements for rural-to-urban migration and temporary residence in the 1958 regulations to ask what conditions governed leaving one's circumstances. Those provisions alone do not describe every period's rural–urban treatment or all restrictions on travel. See [Chapter Five](05-who-owns-the-state.md).

## G34 · Mandatory Provident Fund / MPF（强积金）

Hong Kong's retirement-savings arrangements. Chapter Eight examines recognition and assessment of documents when members seek early withdrawal on grounds of permanent departure. BN(O), British National (Overseas), is the status whose related passports and visas feature in that dispute. Ownership of money and permission to withdraw it early are separate questions. See [Chapter Eight](08-hong-kong.md).

## G35 · Normative claim（规范性主张）

A judgment about how institutions should operate or power should be constrained—for example, that people should be able to withdraw political authorization peacefully. It needs reasons open to discussion, but differs from a claim that an event occurred. The book's institutional proposals should not be read as laws already universally in force. See [Chapter Twelve](12-what-freedom-requires.md).
