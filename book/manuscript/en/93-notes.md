# Notes

*Reader edition v0.2 · 17 September 2026*

These notes follow chapter order and the original source identifiers, providing documents, locations, and limits relevant to the text. These notes summarize the existing chapter research records, without a new review of every external link. Outstanding allegations have not thereby been established. Page numbers generally refer to printed pages unless identified as PDF pages. Translations, transcriptions, and mirrors of one document are not independent witnesses. Chapter Eleven includes supplementary notes P11 and P12.

## Chapter One · Born Unequal

### CH01-B01

Virginia's [December 1662 act](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/primary-documents/negro-womens-children-to-serve-according-to-the-condition-of-the-mother-1662/), reproduced by Encyclopedia Virginia from Hening, *The Statutes at Large*, vol. 2 (1823), p. 170, made a child's status follow the mother's. The transcription and page image establish the legal mechanism, not that every Black child was enslaved. The record gives the legislative session, not a precise enactment day.

### CH01-B02

Justinian, [*Institutes*](https://amesfoundation.law.harvard.edu/digital/CJCiv/JInst.pdf), J. B. Moyle's Oxford 1911 translation as revised in the Harvard Ames Foundation digital edition, Book I, titles 3–5, printed pp. 5–6. These passages define freedom, slavery, free birth, and manumission. Title 4 protects a child's free status when the mother was free during pregnancy. Natural-law language did not itself abolish slavery; status was not wholly immutable.

### CH01-B03

Virginia's [September 1667 act concerning baptism and bondage](https://encyclopediavirginia.org/primary-documents/an-act-declaring-that-baptisme-of-slaves-doth-not-exempt-them-from-bondage-1667/), Hening, vol. 2, p. 260, is reproduced by Encyclopedia Virginia. It states that baptism does not change a person's enslaved or free condition; it does not prohibit baptism. Alongside the 1662 act, it identifies rules preserving status, without establishing particular legislators' motives or individual families' experiences.

### CH01-B04

The French Constitutional Council's [constitutional-block texts](https://qpc360.conseil-constitutionnel.fr/constitutional-block) include the Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen of 26 August 1789. Articles 1 and 6 concern equal rights, distinctions justified by common benefit, and access to public office. They supply a declared standard, not evidence that all social differences disappeared or universal political participation had already been achieved.

### CH01-B05

The Élysée's [Constitution of 3 September 1791](https://www.elysee.fr/la-presidence/la-constitution-du-3-septembre-1791), Title III, chapter I, section II, articles 1, 2, and 6, distinguishes active citizens attending primary assemblies from the electors subsequently chosen. Three days' labor measures the direct-tax qualification, not total property. The chapter selects relevant qualifications; it does not claim to list every condition or reconstruct actual participation by every group.

### CH01-B06

The US National Archives' [Declaration of Independence transcription](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/declaration-transcript), dated 4 July 1776, supplies the passages on rights, government's purpose, and the consent of the governed. This reference introduces Chapter Two's question of authorization. The declaration's principles are distinct from the protection actually available to all people living within the emerging republic.

## Chapter Two · Who Authorizes Government?

### CH02-C01

The [Stamp Act Congress resolutions of 19 October 1765](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/resolu65.asp), transcribed by Yale Avalon, state the delegates' claims concerning taxation, representation, and petition. The [Library of Congress collection entry](https://www.loc.gov/exhibits/creating-the-united-states/revolution-of-the-mind.html#obj12) corroborates the date. Avalon's heading incorrectly calls the body the Continental Congress. These were participating delegates' positions, not proof of unanimous colonial opinion or independent verification of every grievance.

### CH02-C02

The [Declaratory Act of 18 March 1766](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/declaratory_act_1766.asp), reproduced by Yale Avalon from Pickering's statute collection, asserts imperial legislative authority and addresses colonial resolutions denying it. Read the main enactment with section II. The claim concerns the Crown and Parliament, not legislation by the king alone; juxtaposing opposing legal claims does not turn either into an independent judicial finding.

### CH02-C03

The National Archives' [*The Declaration of Independence—A History*](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/declaration-history), especially “The Committee of Five” and “The Engrossed Declaration,” distinguishes the independence resolution of 2 July 1776, adoption of the declaration's text on 4 July, and the subsequent parchment-signing process. It is an archival institution's historical explanation, not a transcript of the Continental Congress's original journal.

### CH02-C04

The National Archives' [4 July 1776 declaration text](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/declaration-transcript) is the source for the preamble and grievances involving representative bodies, judges, and taxation. These statements remain the declaration's allegations unless separately established. Its discussion of changing government includes prudential qualifications. The chapter's analysis of accountability does not independently verify every historical charge listed in the document.

### CH02-C05

John Adams, [*Thoughts on Government*, April 1776](https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Adams/06-04-02-0026-0004), in Founders Online: see the passages on a representative assembly reflecting the people, the dangers of a single assembly, and an independent judiciary. This is a proposed constitutional design, not a constitution universally adopted. Its election and judicial-tenure provisions differ, and no direct copying by Pennsylvania is asserted here.

### CH02-C06

The [Pennsylvania Constitution of 28 September 1776](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/18th_century/pa08.asp), Yale Avalon transcription, establishes a single representative house in the Frame of Government, section 2. Sections 6, 8, and 9 cover voting qualifications, service limits, and annual elections. Section 6 includes residence and taxation requirements with a tax exception for qualifying freeholders' sons. These are not provisions for universal suffrage; distinguish their numbering from the Declaration of Rights.

### CH02-C07

[Abigail Adams to John Adams, 31 March 1776](https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Adams/04-01-02-0241), Founders Online, includes a continuation dated 5 April. The passage beginning “I long to hear” and the following paragraphs address husbands' authority, protection from cruelty, and women's voice in law. This private appeal is not treated as a complete modern suffrage program or as evidence that policy changed because of the letter.

### CH02-C08

[John Adams to Abigail Adams, 14 April 1776](https://founders.archives.gov/documents/Adams/04-01-02-0248), Founders Online, supplies the reply concerning new laws and the existing male order. Its joking and ironic epistolary tone matters. The chapter analyzes this response rather than presenting it as a congressional decision, a complete statement of Adams's thought, or the position and motives of all men.

### CH02-C09

[*Freedom Petition Transcripts*, document 11](https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/web.sas.upenn.edu/dist/6/824/files/2023/12/Freedom-Petition-Transcripts_04252024-5921cca8e2c94c80.pdf), edited by Grant Stanton and hosted by Early American Studies, PDF pp. 16–17, records the petition dated 13 January 1777; compare the [Wisconsin transcription](https://csac.history.wisc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/281/2020/12/Readings-session-1_-Emancipation-Documents-from-the-Revolutionary-Era.pdf), pp. 2–3. Lancaster Hill and Prince Hall are among the signatories. The freedom request and children's age-twenty-one condition were proposals, not enacted law. The petition is distinct from its draft, the proposed bill, and the 18 March processing record.

## Chapter Three · Slaveholders in a Free Republic

### CH03-A01

[Jourdon Anderson's letter of 7 August 1865](https://www.americanyawp.com/reader/reconstruction/jourdon-anderson-writes-his-former-master-1865/), American Yawp transcription, comes from Lydia Maria Child's *The Freedmen's Book* (1865), pp. 265–267; the [Gutenberg edition](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/38479/38479-h/38479-h.htm) preserves pagination and the dictation note. The $11,680 is Anderson's wage calculation, with interest and deductions separately proposed, not a judgment or payment. His recollections, accusations, and concerns for his children remain attributed to the letter.

### CH03-A02

The [original 1787 US Constitution](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/constitution-transcript), National Archives transcription, supplies Article I, sections 2, 9, and 10, and Article IV, section 2, on apportionment, titles of nobility, and the return of persons escaping service. The [National Park Service discussion](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/legal-underpinnings.htm) clarifies counting enslaved people without enfranchising them. Three-fifths was an apportionment formula, not fractional individual votes or human worth; historical provisions are not presented as current law.

### CH03-A03

South Carolina's [*Declaration of the Immediate Causes*, 24 December 1860](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/19th_century/csa_scarsec.asp), Yale Avalon transcription, invokes 1776 and the fugitive-slave clause before alleging breaches by nonslaveholding states. Its date differs from the 20 December secession ordinance. The document establishes secessionists' publicly stated reasons, not the truth of each accusation or the motives of every resident and soldier.

### CH03-A04

Texas's [*Declaration of Causes*, 2 February 1861](https://www.tsl.texas.gov/ref/abouttx/secession/2feb1861.html), Texas State Library transcription, derives from Winkler's *Journal of the Secession Convention of Texas 1861* (1912), pp. 61–65. The passages beginning “She was received,” “We hold,” and “That in this free government” defend slavery and restrict equality. Other stated reasons include frontier defense; the document's position is not a verified account of every grievance.

### CH03-A05

[Lincoln to Horace Greeley, 22 August 1862](https://housedivided.dickinson.edu/sites/lincoln/letter-to-horace-greeley-august-22-1862/), Dickinson College's searchable transcription, distinguishes the president's official objective from his concluding personal wish for universal freedom. Both belong in interpreting the letter. Modern commentary on the page is not Lincoln's speech, and the chapter reads the letter alongside the earlier emancipation draft rather than inferring a sudden change of mind from publication order.

### CH03-A06

The National Archives' [*The Preliminary Emancipation Proclamation, 1862*](https://www.archives.gov/exhibits/american_originals_iv/sections/text_preliminary_emancipation.html) explains that Lincoln read a draft to his cabinet in July and issued the preliminary proclamation on 22 September. It is the archival institution's historical account, not a verbatim cabinet record. This chronology limits inferences drawn solely from the August Greeley letter and the September announcement.

### CH03-A07

The National Archives provides the [Emancipation Proclamation and historical explanation](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/emancipation-proclamation), with a [separate transcription](https://www.archives.gov/exhibits/featured-documents/emancipation-proclamation/transcript.html), dated 1 January 1863. Read the wartime authority, specified rebellious territories, exemptions, federal commitment to freedom, and armed-service provisions together. The proclamation did not abolish slavery everywhere that day; territorial limits and military enforcement likewise do not make it meaningless. It was not the beginning of all Black military service.

### CH03-A08

[James Henry Gooding to Lincoln, 28 September 1863](https://www.freedmen.umd.edu/gooding.htm), Freedmen and Southern Society Project transcription, discusses unequal pay, soldiers' common status, and family fuel needs; the archival reference is NARA RG 94, Colored Troops Division, H-133 1863. [DocsTeach, identifier 594707](https://docsteach.org/document/letter-corporal-gooding-to-president-lincoln/), corroborates his corporal's rank and regiment. The letter is not evidence that Congress changed the law because of it, nor independent proof of every collective biographical claim.

### CH03-A09

The National Archives' [Thirteenth Amendment text and history](https://www.archives.gov/milestone-documents/13th-amendment) distinguishes congressional passage on 31 January 1865 from ratification on 6 December. Section 1 retains the punishment exception only for a person duly convicted; section 2 grants congressional enforcement power. The existence of that exception does not by itself establish the constitutionality of any later state labor or criminal statute.

### CH03-A10

[*The Mississippi Black Code, 1865*](https://cii.wwu.edu/showcase2001/richards/resources/black_code.pdf), a one-page extract hosted by Western Washington University, contains five passages on vagrancy, hiring out after unpaid fines, documentation, labor contracts, and interference with employment. The wage-forfeiture provision concerns specified Black workers' written contracts exceeding one month. Age, starting date, and “without good cause” qualifications matter. This extract does not establish all enforcement, its scale, or judicial rulings on constitutionality.

### CH03-A11

The National Archives' [amendments transcription](https://www.archives.gov/founding-docs/amendments-11-27) supplies the Fourteenth Amendment, section 1, ratified 9 July 1868, and the Fifteenth Amendment, section 1, ratified 3 February 1870. Citizenship includes birth or naturalization and jurisdiction conditions; due process and equal protection cover persons. The Fifteenth forbids specified grounds of voting discrimination rather than abolishing every qualification. Constitutional enactment is not evidence of universal practical enforcement.

## Chapter Four · In the Name of Equality

### CH04-EQ01

Alexandra Kollontai, [*The Workers Opposition in Russia* (1921)](https://www.marxists.org/archive/kollonta/1921/workers-opp-alt/index.html), MIA transcription of the Chicago IWW edition: [“On Bureaucracy and Self-Activity of the Masses”](https://www.marxists.org/archive/kollonta/1921/workers-opp-alt/5-bureaucracy.htm) supplies the canteen, nursery, firewood, appointment, and expression examples; the [trade-union chapter](https://www.marxists.org/archive/kollonta/1921/workers-opp-alt/4-tus.htm) clarifies her organizational proposal. These are Kollontai's examples and judgments; they have not been individually checked against administrative records. Her continued party-leadership framework precludes treating her as a modern multiparty liberal.

### CH04-EQ02

Marx and Engels, [*Manifesto of the Communist Party*, chapter I (1848)](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1848/communist-manifesto/ch01.htm), MIA English transcription, is used for class struggle, the bourgeoisie's revolutionary role, the world market, wage labor, and factory discipline. These are Marx and Engels's historical propositions. The definitions and recorded-history qualification in Engels's later notes were not part of the original 1848 wording.

### CH04-EQ03

The *Manifesto*'s [chapter II](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1848/communist-manifesto/ch02.htm) distinguishes bourgeois property from personal appropriation and discusses concentrated productive resources, ten measures, and individual development. [Chapter IV](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1848/communist-manifesto/ch04.htm) ends with a forcible-overthrow argument. In the relevant chapter II passage, the state is the proletariat organized as the ruling class, not automatically any later party apparatus. These arguments alone do not establish causation for a particular subsequent atrocity.

### CH04-EQ04

Marx, [*Critique of the Gotha Programme*](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1875/gotha/index.htm), written in April or early May 1875 and first published in abridged form in 1891; the MIA translation follows *Selected Works*, vol. 3 (1970), pp. 13–30. [Part I](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1875/gotha/ch01.htm) addresses deductions and stages of distribution; [Part IV](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1875/gotha/ch04.htm) addresses society, state, and transition. Conditions on abundance remain important. Questions about transitional accountability are the book's analysis, not procedures already supplied by Marx.

### CH04-EQ05

Marx, [*The Civil War in France*, part III (1871)](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1871/civil-war-france/ch05.htm), provides his interpretation of election, recall, official pay, and public power in the Commune, rather than an independent implementation audit. The [1872 German preface to the *Manifesto*](https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1848/communist-manifesto/preface.htm#preface-1872), dated 24 June in London, retains general principles while qualifying measures and rejecting simple takeover of the existing state machine. It does not renounce revolution or reduce the program to ordinary welfare policy.

### CH04-EQ06

Lenin, [*What Is To Be Done?*](https://www.marxists.org/archive/lenin/works/1901/witbd/index.htm), written in 1901–1902 and published in March 1902, MIA translation from *Collected Works*, vol. 5, pp. 347–530. [Chapter II, A and B, note 15](https://www.marxists.org/archive/lenin/works/1901/witbd/ii.htm), includes workers' capacity to become theorists; [IV, C–E](https://www.marxists.org/archive/lenin/works/1901/witbd/iv.htm), distinguishes broad worker organizations from professional revolutionaries under tsarist policing. Underground organizational arguments are not a record of later governing practice.

### CH04-EQ07

Lenin, [*The State and Revolution*](https://www.marxists.org/archive/lenin/works/1917/staterev/index.htm), written August–September 1917 and first published in 1918, MIA edition citing *Collected Works*, vol. 25, pp. 381–492. [Chapter III, sections 2–3](https://www.marxists.org/archive/lenin/works/1917/staterev/ch03.htm), addresses election, recall, pay, and representatives' work; [V, sections 2–4](https://www.marxists.org/archive/lenin/works/1917/staterev/ch05.htm), combines majority democracy with coercion of exploiters and conditions on the state's disappearance. The text contains both antibureaucratic promises and coercive commitments; neither proves implementation.

### CH04-EQ08

Rosa Luxemburg, [*The Russian Revolution*](https://www.marxists.org/archive/luxemburg/1918/russian-revolution/index.htm), written in 1918 and first published by Paul Levi in 1922; MIA uses Bertram Wolfe's 1940 translation. [Chapter VI](https://www.marxists.org/archive/luxemburg/1918/russian-revolution/ch06.htm) discusses dissent, public life, and bureaucracy; [VIII](https://www.marxists.org/archive/luxemburg/1918/russian-revolution/ch08.htm) preserves her revolutionary commitments and the context of war and isolation. Her criticisms remain attributed judgments, not independent verification of every measure or number she mentions.

### CH04-EQ09

The RCP(B) Tenth Congress's adopted [*On Party Unity*, 16 March 1921](https://www.marxists.org/history/ussr/government/party-congress/10th/16.htm), MIA transcription, recognizes criticism in paragraph 4, addresses bureaucracy in paragraph 5, and orders faction dissolution in paragraph 6. The following unnumbered passage specifies disciplinary powers and exceptional voting requirements. This is the adopted text, not Lenin's preliminary draft. Its charges against opponents remain partisan claims; restricting factions is not literally prohibiting every criticism.

## Chapter Five · After the Revolution, Who Owns the State?

### CH05-S01

Yang Jisheng's [《永久的墓碑》, the preface to *Tombstone*](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/225670.html), reprinted by China Digital Times on 10 May 2012, supplies his recollection of Yang Xiushen's death in 1959 and identifies him as both uncle and adoptive father. An [NPR interview, 10 November 2012](https://www.npr.org/2012/11/10/164732497/a-grim-chronicle-of-chinas-great-famine), offers comparison. These are later recollections, not contemporaneous death records; the chapter does not adopt the preface's national mortality estimates.

### CH05-S02

Mao Zedong, *On the People's Democratic Dictatorship*, 30 June 1949, in [Chinese](https://www.marxists.org/chinese/maozedong/marxist.org-chinese-mao-19490630.htm) and the [*Selected Works*, vol. 4, English translation](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/mao/selected-works/volume-4/mswv4_65.htm). The relevant passages define “the people,” coercive state organs, and working-class leadership through the Communist Party. The national bourgeoisie's place in the contemporary alliance remains important. Political enemy categories are not proof of individual crimes or of advance planning for every later disaster.

### CH05-S03

The [PRC Constitution adopted on 20 September 1954](https://news.12371.cn/2015/03/18/ARTI1426665514681575.shtml), republished on the Communist Party member portal in 2015, provides articles 2, 17–18, 27–28, 78, 90, and 97 on public power, supervision, appointments, courts, movement, complaint, and compensation. Articles 5–10 retain differing property forms alongside transformation requirements. The constitution contained these rights provisions despite failures in implementation; their existence does not demonstrate effective protection.

### CH05-S04

Mao's [《反对党内的资产阶级思想》](https://www.marxists.org/chinese/maozedong/marxist.org-chinese-mao-19530812.htm), a speech of 12 August 1953, follows *Selected Works*, vol. 5 (1977), pp. 90–97. The paragraph on concentration and decentralization assigns major decisions to party committees and implementation to government while retaining collective discussion and delegated lesser matters. It does not say the center decides every detail. The separate November cadre-management decision was not available in full and supplies no additional personnel rules here.

### CH05-S05

The [Government Administration Council grain-purchase and supply order](https://cn.govopendata.com/renminribao/1954/03/01/1/) was adopted on 19 November 1953 and dated 23 November; the linked transcription is from *People's Daily*, 1 March 1954, p. 1. Articles 1, 3, and 6 cover state quantities and prices, local assessment, and private-trader restrictions, while retaining disposal of surplus after obligations. The text establishes institutional design, not every famine-era practice or criminal punishment.

### CH05-S06

The [1958 Household Registration Regulations](https://platform.who.int/docs/default-source/mca-documents/policy-documents/law/CHN-CC-55-01-LAW-1958-chn-Regulations-on-Household-Registration.pdf), adopted and promulgated on 9 January, are preserved as a Chinese PDF in WHO's policy library. Article 10, PDF p. 3, requires specified documentation for rural-to-urban transfer; articles 15–16, pp. 4–5, concern temporary residence. WHO hosts the document rather than endorsing the policy. These requirements do not amount to a ban on every journey or prove every reported obstruction of famine migration.

### CH05-S07

Peng Dehuai's [《为什么要写信给毛主席》](https://www.dswxyjy.org.cn/n/2015/0610/c244516-27134656.html), republished in 2015 from notes made around the July–August 1959 Lushan meeting, explains his evidence and difficulties raising objections. The separate [14 July letter transcription](https://www.robertsuettinger.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Chapter-4-FN-112-Peng-Dehuai-Letter-to-Mao-in-1959-Lushan.pdf), PDF pp. 1–5, is not a manuscript facsimile. Both retain support for the Great Leap and commune direction. The explanatory notes are not the letter itself, and neither text calls for the Party's overthrow.

### CH05-S08

The CCP's [Resolution on Certain Questions in the History of Our Party Since the Founding of the PRC](https://www.12371.cn/2021/11/09/ARTI1636454249781129.shtml), adopted 27 June 1981, paragraphs 17–20, acknowledges errors in the Lushan criticism and Anti-Right-Deviation campaign and discusses the Cultural Revolution. It also describes later correction and distinguishes Mao's errors from Mao Zedong Thought. This retrospective party judgment is not a comprehensive independent investigation, a mortality count, or unanimous victims' assessment.

### CH05-S09

Yang Jisheng, [*Tombstone: The Great Chinese Famine, 1958–1962*, publisher's chapter-one extract](https://us.macmillan.com/books/9780374533991/tombstone/), English translation by Stacy Mosher and Guo Jian (2012; paperback 2013), describes Li Ruiying, Jigongshan, Lu Xianwen, false output reports, and procurement. A [reprint of Zhang Shufan's Xinyang account](https://www.wyzxwk.com/Article/lishi/2009/09/64352.html) helped check Chinese names. Yang's investigation remains attributed; its underlying local archives were not independently inspected, and it does not complete the causal history of his adoptive father's death.

### CH05-S10

[VOA's report, updated 30 January 2014](https://www.voachinese.com/a/the-aplogy-from-red-guard-song-binbin-20140114/1829538.html), quotes Wang Jingyao's 27 January statement concerning Bian Zhongyun's killing on 5 August 1966 and his request for investigation. The [Blinken Open Society Archives catalogue for Hu Jie's *Though I Am Gone* (2006)](https://catalog.archivum.org/catalog/koj6zOoO), HU_OSA_00003186, describes preserved photographs. The film and original photographs were not examined. The cited materials do not establish that Wang witnessed the entire assault, and later apologies do not establish every student's criminal responsibility.

### CH05-S11

The [Sixteen Points decision of 8 August 1966](https://www.massline.org/PekingReview/PR1966/PR1966-33g.htm) is transcribed from *Peking Review*, vol. 9, no. 33, 12 August, pp. 6–11. Articles 4, 6, and 9 concern mass initiative, reasoning rather than coercion within the stated political category, and party-led organizations with election and replacement provisions. The [Columbia extract](https://afe.easia.columbia.edu/ps/cup/sixteen_points.pdf) includes only articles 1, 2, and 9. Bian Zhongyun's death preceded this decision; the text cannot be cited as an unrestricted authorization to kill.

### CH05-S12

Deng Xiaoping, [*On the Reform of the System of Party and State Leadership*, 18 August 1980](https://www.marxists.org/reference/archive/deng-xiaoping/1980/220.htm), MIA English transcription, parts I–IV. Relevant passages address personnel proposals, political qualifications, concentrated power and institutional defects, public supervision, and help given to officials' relatives. The translator is unidentified. A reform proposal and an admission of problems do not demonstrate completed reform or public control over the ruling organization's choices.

## Chapter Six · Open Markets and Political Continuity

### CH06-R01

The China Agricultural Museum's transcription and explanation of the [Xiaogang household-contract agreement](https://wwj.beijing.gov.cn/bjww/362760/362767/bwgxh/spzl/326039637/index.html), hosted by Beijing's cultural heritage authority, support the production, delivery, and child-support commitments. The chapter uses late 1978 because the written month and later reconstruction differ. The original document was not inspected; handprint counts and room details are omitted. Anticipated punishment in the agreement is not evidence that the signatories later received that punishment.

### CH06-R02

The [Third Plenum communiqué of 22 December 1978](https://fuwu.12371.cn/2012/09/25/ARTI1348557244563110.shtml), republished from Xinhua, combines greater economic autonomy with unified planning and party leadership. Part II's agricultural discussion retains three-level ownership with the team as its basis; part III advocates institutionalized democracy, legality, and opposition to privilege above law. The document neither fully establishes household contracting nationwide nor proves that its political and economic proposals were implemented.

### CH06-R03

The [party-history chronology](https://cpc.people.com.cn/GB/64162/64164/4416121.html) dates the forwarding of the 1982 rural-work conference minutes to 1 January. The [public text reprinted by Sina](https://finance.sina.cn/sa/2010-02-01/detail-ikftpnny0527406.d.html?from=wap) opens with Xinhua's 5 April dispatch. Items II, IV, VI, VIII, XXIV, and XXV recognize responsibility systems while retaining collective land, contractual duties, planning, procurement, and local organizational functions. This published version is not a facsimile of the January circular; central recognition was not land privatization or simultaneous nationwide implementation.

### CH06-R04

The World Bank and China's Development Research Center jointly authored [*Four Decades of Poverty Reduction in China* (2022)](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/bdadc16a4f5c1c88a839c0f905cde802-0070012022/original/Poverty-Synthesis-Report-final.pdf), pp. xiii–xv, 2–3, and 13–14. The nearly 800 million estimate spans roughly four decades and uses the then $1.90 international poverty line at 2011 purchasing-power parity. It is one joint estimate, not two independent findings; underlying survey data were not recalculated. The report does not show that every political arrangement was necessary for poverty reduction.

### CH06-R05

Deng Xiaoping's [*Uphold the Four Cardinal Principles*, 30 March 1979](https://afe.easia.columbia.edu/main_pop/ps/ps_china-deng-xiaoping-uphold-principles.htm), appears in Columbia's Asia for Educators excerpt from *Sources of Chinese Tradition*, second edition, vol. 2 (2000), pp. 492–493. The speech lists the principles and presents them as modernization's conditions. The surrounding teaching introduction is separate from Deng's words, and his characterization of critics' motives is not independent evidence of those motives.

### CH06-R06

The [original 1982 PRC Constitution](https://news.12371.cn/2015/03/18/ARTI1426664689523381.shtml), adopted and promulgated on 4 December, is reproduced separately from later amendments. Articles 60, 79, 87, and 93–94 distinguish institutional terms and particular consecutive-term limits; articles 5, 35, 41, and 62–63 concern legality, rights, appointment, and removal. Presidential and certain government-office limits did not apply automatically to the Party general secretary or military chair. Formal accountability provisions are not proof of effective enforcement.

### CH06-R07

None of these texts by itself proves effective implementation of the rights it expresses.

### CH12-L02

The [*Universal Declaration of Human Rights*](https://india.un.org/en/download/148244/253557), adopted 10 December 1948, is reproduced in the UN's 2017 accessible edition, linked from its [2023 publication page](https://india.un.org/en/253557-accessible-universal-declaration-human-rights); compare the [Minnesota transcription](https://hrlibrary.umn.edu/instree/b1udhr.htm). Articles 7, 8, 10, and 19–21 address equality, remedies, fair hearing, expression, association, and political participation; articles 29–30 preserve duties and limits on destructive readings. It supplies a normative standard, not automatically enforceable domestic law everywhere or a requirement that incumbents lose every election.

### CH12-L03

The [*International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights*](https://2covenants.ohchr.org/About-ICCPR.html), adopted 16 December 1966 and in force from 23 March 1976, is compared with the [Minnesota transcription](https://hrlibrary.umn.edu/instree/b3ccpr.htm). Article 2(3) concerns remedies and enforcement; 14 fair proceedings; 25 citizens' participation; 26 equality for persons. Article 4 conditions emergency derogations on formal proclamation, strict necessity, protected rights, and notification. Fixed sunset periods are this book's proposal, not its wording. No country-by-country treaty-status or domestic-case conclusion is offered.

### CH12-L04

[*United States v. Nixon*, 418 U.S. 683 (1974)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USREPORTS-418/pdf/USREPORTS-418-683.pdf), decided 24 July, recognizes presumptive presidential confidentiality at pp. 705–708 but requires it to yield to demonstrated criminal-trial evidentiary need at p. 713. Pages 714–716 require in-camera review and protection of irrelevant or inadmissible material. Footnote 19, p. 712, excludes civil discovery, congressional demands, and state secrets. The decision did not convict Nixon or abolish all privilege. The reading covers printed pp. 683–693 and 698–716.

### CH12-L05

The [*Basic Principles on the Independence of the Judiciary*](https://hrlibrary.umn.edu/instree/i5bpij.htm), adopted at the 1985 Milan UN crime-prevention congress, are reproduced by Minnesota from A/CONF.121/22/Rev.1, p. 59. Principles 1–2, 7, 10–13, and 17–20 address independence, resources, qualifications, tenure, and discipline. They are principles, not another treaty or proof of enforcement. They prescribe no single appointment model and preserve lawful review and removal procedures rather than making judges unaccountable.

### CH12-L06

The [*United Nations Convention against Corruption*](https://treaties.un.org/doc/source/RecentTexts/Corruption_E.pdf), adopted 31 October 2003 and in force from 14 December 2005, as confirmed by the [treaty register](https://treaties.un.org/Pages/ViewDetails.aspx?src=TREATY&mtdsg_no=XVIII-14&chapter=18&clang=_en), articles 7–10 (PDF pp. 6–10), addresses political funding, conflicts, declarations, procurement, public finance, and transparency. Wording varies among considering, endeavoring, and adopting measures, with domestic-law qualifications. These provisions are not identical unconditional duties, proof of universal implementation, or evidence that a particular official is corrupt.

### CH12-L07

UN General Assembly [resolution A/RES/60/147](https://documents.un.org/api/symbol/access?l=en&s=A%2FRES%2F60%2F147&t=pdf), adopted 16 December 2005 and distributed 21 March 2006, annexes the Basic Principles on remedy and reparation for gross human-rights and serious humanitarian-law violations. Paragraphs 9, 12–13, 17–24, and 27 address victim status, safe access, claims, enforcement, reparation, information, and accused persons' rights. The preamble states that these principles create no new obligations. Public truth must respect safety; access to remedies is not unrestricted access to every file or automatic compensation. The English nine-page text was read in full.

### CH12-L08

The publicly available [《中国大陆自由宣言》](https://github.com/my-freedom-foundation/declarations/blob/main/mainland-china-liberty/cn/dec-simplified.md) and [*Mainland China Liberty Declaration*](https://github.com/my-freedom-foundation/declarations/blob/main/mainland-china-liberty/en/dec.md), referred to here as the 2022 declaration, state equality, shared authorization, withdrawal of authority, and choosing a government that protects rights. Neither current text has a date line; the 2022 Git-history version was not authenticated. Chinese lines 6–8, 35, 37 and English lines 13–23, 96–111 support the attribution. This book's procedures and closing commitments are further proposals, not retroactive quotations; the declaration's factual allegations remain individually subject to verification.
