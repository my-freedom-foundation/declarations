# Appendix A　A Select Chronology

*In the People's Name: Privilege, Revolution, and Unaccountable Power · English draft v0.2 · 17 September 2026*

These thirty-five entries place the book's discussion of status, consent, organizational power, and public accountability in time. Dates retain the precision supported by the chapters and shared source records. Events, adoption of texts, later recollections, and release of records are identified separately. Each entry links to the relevant source record, which provides the underlying document and its scope.

| ID | Date | Event and its place in the book | Source locator |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| C01 | December 1662 | Virginia's colonial legislature provided that children born there would be free or enslaved according to their mother's condition. Legal status could sustain domination across generations. | [Chapter 1, B01](93-notes.md#ch01-b01) |
| C02 | 19 October 1765 | The Stamp Act Congress claimed that colonial taxation required consent given personally or through representatives, and should proceed through local representative assemblies. These were the participating delegates' claims of right. | [Chapter 2, C01](93-notes.md#ch02-c01) |
| C03 | 2 and 4 July 1776 | The Continental Congress passed the independence resolution on 2 July and adopted the Declaration's text on 4 July. The Declaration linked just government to the consent of the governed. Adoption of the text was distinct from the subsequent signing process for the parchment document. | [Chapter 2, C03](93-notes.md#ch02-c03), [C04](93-notes.md#ch02-c04) |
| C04 | 13 January 1777 | A petition signed by Lancaster Hill, Prince Hall, and others asked the Massachusetts Bay legislature for freedom for enslaved people. The principle of liberty became a challenge to the society that had declared independence; the petition was not an enacted law. | [Chapter 2, C09](93-notes.md#ch02-c09) |
| C05 | 26 August 1789 | France's Declaration of the Rights of Man and of the Citizen set out principles of equal rights and public benefit, providing a public standard against which distinctions of status could be judged. | [Chapter 1, B04](93-notes.md#ch01-b04) |
| C06 | 3 September 1791 | The French Constitution specified qualifications for “active citizens” and the selection of electors. Direct-tax and status requirements for participation in primary assemblies exposed a gap between declared equality and equal political participation. | [Chapter 1, B05](93-notes.md#ch01-b05) |
| C07 | 1848 | Marx and Engels's Communist Manifesto explained social conflict through class relations and proposed changes to ownership and the centralization of productive resources. The book asks how people controlling those resources on workers' behalf would remain answerable to workers. | [Chapter 4, EQ02](93-notes.md#ch04-eq02), [EQ03](93-notes.md#ch04-eq03) |
| C08 | 1 January 1863 | Lincoln issued the Emancipation Proclamation under wartime authority, declaring enslaved people in designated rebellious areas free and pledging federal support. Territorial exceptions and the need for military protection meant it did not unconditionally abolish slavery throughout the country that day. | [Chapter 3, A07](93-notes.md#ch03-a07) |
| C09 | 7 August 1865 | Jourdon Anderson's letter to his former enslaver presented a calculation of wages for past labor and asked about his children's safety and education. It records his demands, not the payment of compensation. | [Chapter 3, A01](93-notes.md#ch03-a01) |
| C10 | 6 December 1865 | The Thirteenth Amendment reached the required number of state ratifications, following congressional passage on 31 January. It prohibited slavery and involuntary servitude, retained an exception for punishment following due conviction, and gave Congress enforcement authority. | [Chapter 3, A09](93-notes.md#ch03-a09) |
| C11 | 16 March 1921 | The Tenth Congress of the Russian Communist Party (Bolsheviks) adopted its resolution On Party Unity, requiring the dissolution of factions while specifying channels for criticism. Chapter Four closes with the tension between organizational unity and workers' independent expression. | [Chapter 4, EQ09](93-notes.md#ch04-eq09) |
| C12 | 10 December 1948 | The United Nations adopted the Universal Declaration of Human Rights, setting out standards including equality, effective remedies, fair hearings, and political participation. The book uses it as a normative reference for judging power. | [Chapter 12, L02](93-notes.md#ch12-l02) |
| C13 | 30 June 1949 | Mao Zedong's On the People's Democratic Dictatorship set out political categories of “the people,” Communist Party leadership, and coercion against enemies. Who could define the people becomes an opening question about the new political order. | [Chapter 5, S02](93-notes.md#ch05-s02) |
| C14 | 19 November 1953 | The Government Administration Council adopted an order establishing planned grain purchase and supply, specifying grain types, prices, quantities, and assignments through administrative levels. The text itself is dated 23 November. These provisions directly connected state allocation with household livelihoods. | [Chapter 5, S05](93-notes.md#ch05-s05) |
| C15 | 20 September 1954 | The PRC Constitution was adopted, including provisions on popular power, public supervision, independent adjudication, freedom of movement and residence, complaints, and compensation. Written promises and people's ability to use rights require separate examination. | [Chapter 5, S03](93-notes.md#ch05-s03) |
| C16 | 9 January 1958 | The household-registration regulations were adopted and promulgated. Procedures for rural residents moving to cities required proof of employment, school admission, or permission to move. This was not a prohibition on all travel. | [Chapter 5, S06](93-notes.md#ch05-s06) |
| C17 | 14 July 1959 | Peng Dehuai wrote to Mao about problems in the Great Leap Forward. He still supported the direction of the Leap and communes; his letter and later notes express the position of an internal critic seeking policy correction. | [Chapter 5, S07](93-notes.md#ch05-s07) |
| C18 | 5 and 8 August 1966 | Wang Jingyao's later statement recounts that Bian Zhongyun was beaten to death at the girls' middle school attached to Beijing Normal University on 5 August. On 8 August the CCP adopted the Cultural Revolution's Sixteen Points, which combined Party leadership with provisions for reasoning and protecting differing views within the people. Her death preceded that decision. | [Chapter 5, S10](93-notes.md#ch05-s10), [S11](93-notes.md#ch05-s11) |
| C19 | 24 July 1974 | In United States v. Nixon, the Supreme Court held that the President's general confidentiality claim could not defeat a demonstrated, specific need for evidence in the pending criminal trial. The material had to go first to the District Court for private review. The ruling illustrates bounded, enforceable judicial constraint. | [Chapter 12, L04](93-notes.md#ch12-l04) |
| C20 | 22 December and late 1978 | The CCP Third Plenum communiqué adopted on 22 December proposed greater economic autonomy while retaining unified planning and Party leadership. Xiaogang's household farming agreement dates to late 1978; the book does not establish its exact signing day. The available records establish neither their sequence nor that they formed a single policy. | [Chapter 6, R01](93-notes.md#ch06-r01), [R02](93-notes.md#ch06-r02) |
| C21 | 27 June 1981 | The CCP adopted its Resolution on Certain Questions in the History of Our Party Since the Founding of the People's Republic of China, acknowledging errors in the Lushan criticism, the anti-right-deviation campaign, and the Cultural Revolution. This was the Party's retrospective assessment, not an independent investigation of every victim's experience. | [Chapter 5, S08](93-notes.md#ch05-s08) |
| C22 | 1 January and 4 December 1982 | On 1 January the central leadership endorsed rural-work conference minutes recognizing several forms of production responsibility while retaining collective land ownership. The Constitution adopted and promulgated on 4 December imposed a two-consecutive-term ceiling on specified offices, including the state presidency and vice-presidency. That ceiling did not apply to the Party general secretary or military commission chair. | [Chapter 6, R03](93-notes.md#ch06-r03), [R06](93-notes.md#ch06-r06) |
| C23 | 1984 | The Sino-British Joint Declaration arranged Hong Kong's handover and set out basic policies concerning autonomy, residents' rights, and economic institutions. The book compares state commitments with rights residents can require institutions to honor. | [Chapter 8, HK03](93-notes.md#ch08-hk03) |
| C24 | 3–4 June 1989 | Troops advanced along western roads toward central Beijing and fired on civilians. The US Embassy's 22 June cable records those actions through material of differing directness. Ding Zilin's testimony recounts her son Jiang Jielian being shot at Muxidi late on 3 June; she did not witness the shooting. | [Chapter 6, R09](93-notes.md#ch06-r09), [R10](93-notes.md#ch06-r10) |
| C25 | 1 July 1997 | Hong Kong's handover took place. The Joint Declaration specified this date; the Queen's Christmas broadcast that year recalled the handover and the Prince of Wales's representative role. The change in sovereignty did not itself settle how residents could constrain their rulers. | [Chapter 8, HK03](93-notes.md#ch08-hk03), [HK01](93-notes.md#ch08-hk01) |
| C26 | 11 March 2018 | A constitutional amendment removed the two-consecutive-term ceiling for the state president and vice-president while retaining terms matching those of the National People's Congress. It did not abolish terms for every office or directly confer a lifetime position. | [Chapter 7, F09](93-notes.md#ch07-f09) |
| C27 | 30 December 2019 and 3 January 2020 | According to the subsequent investigation report, Li Wenliang shared preliminary “SARS” information on 30 December, adding that evening that the virus was still being typed. Police admonished him on 3 January. While the warning still required verification, its messenger already faced personal consequences. | [Chapter 10, T01](93-notes.md#ch10-t01) |
| C28 | 19 March 2020 | The National Supervisory Commission's investigation team recommended withdrawing Li Wenliang's admonition. Wuhan police decided that evening to withdraw it and apologize to his family. Recommendation and withdrawal were distinct steps; Li had died on 7 February. | [Chapter 10, T01](93-notes.md#ch10-t01) |
| C29 | 30 June and 7 July 2020 | Hong Kong's National Security Law took effect on 30 June, and the implementation rules for Article 43 on 7 July. Chapter Eight examines asset-freezing powers alongside the avenues for court revocation and permissions affecting individuals and institutions. | [Chapter 8, HK11](93-notes.md#ch08-hk11) |
| C30 | 24 June 2021 | Apple Daily ceased publication. Next Digital's 5 September board statement connected the closure to restrictions on funds and operating payments; a Hong Kong government letter of 8 July defended the legal basis and suspicions behind the freezes. Each is an account by a party to the dispute. | [Chapter 8, HK12](93-notes.md#ch08-hk12), [HK13](93-notes.md#ch08-hk13) |
| C31 | 10 March 2023 | Xi Jinping was elected state president for a third consecutive term. This state election is distinct from the Party appointments of October 2022; the succession of terms does not itself establish a personal plan to rule for life. | [Chapter 7, F10](93-notes.md#ch07-f10), [F08](93-notes.md#ch07-f08) |
| C32 | 12 December 2024 | The public judgment in H6 upheld his exclusion, examining access to the Duke of York, commercial authorization, and risks of influence. It reviewed a national-security decision; it was not a criminal conviction for espionage. | [Chapter 9, I06](93-notes.md#ch09-i06) |
| C33 | 29 January 2025 | The AMARVEL verdict sheet bears this date. Qingzhou Wang and Yiyi Chen were found guilty of precursor-import conspiracy and money-laundering conspiracy, but not guilty on a separate fentanyl and related-substances conspiracy count. This entry follows the verdict sheet; the later DOJ announcement gives a different verdict date. The findings did not establish direction by senior state leaders. | [Chapter 9, I01](93-notes.md#ch09-i01) |
| C34 | 18 June 2026 | The US Office of the Director of National Intelligence released records including internal discussion and differing views in 2020 concerning Dr. Li-Meng Yan's report. This is the release date. Institutional discussion does not establish that allegations of deliberate release or a personal order were verified. | [Chapter 11, P03](93-notes.md#ch11-p03) |
| C35 | 18 August 2026 | David Morens's guilty plea was announced, and a signed stipulation of facts was filed that day. It recorded arrangements to conceal official records and participation. The plea material supports discussion of those acts; it is neither a finding of deliberate pandemic release nor a sentencing record. | [Chapter 10, T07](93-notes.md#ch10-t07), [Chapter 11, P08](93-notes.md#ch11-p08) |

Sequence helps identify connections worth investigating and rule out mistaken chronologies. Explaining causation still requires the chapters' examination of actors, interests, authority, and evidence.
